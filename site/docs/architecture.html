DGM Mathematical Formulation & Architecture

Step 1: Polar Domain Geometry & Valley Confinement

The delta apex is positioned at origin $(r=0, \theta=0)$. The marine shoreline boundary is located at radius $r = R_{\text{shore}}$ (default $10,000 \text{ m}$). Marine hydrodynamic energy expands active sectoral fan spreading, while valley confinement narrows it:

$$\theta_{\text{open}}(R_{\text{rt}}, C_{\text{conf}}) = (\theta_{\text{base}} + \Delta \theta \cdot R_{\text{rt}}) \cdot (1 - C_{\text{conf}})$$

Lateral valley corridor boundaries clamp Cartesian coordinate excursions: $$|x(r)| \le \frac{W_{\text{valley}}}{2}$$

Step 2: Polar Tree Backbone Growth & Shoreward Spatial Scaling

A directed branching tree is grown outwards from apex. Branching order $k \in {1, 2, 3}$ is sampled stochastically according to branching_weights.

  • Shoreward Node Contraction: Inter-junction distance contracts as channels approach the sea: $$\Delta r(r) = \Delta r_0 \cdot \max(0.2, 1 - \lambda_{\text{node}} \cdot r / r_{\text{shore}})$$
  • Coastal Sinuosity Straightening: Distributaries straighten in the seaward reach: $$S(r) = \max\left(1.0, 1.0 + (S_0 - 1.0) \cdot \max\left(0, 1 - \lambda_{\text{sin}} \cdot \frac{r_{\text{mid}}}{r_{\text{shore}}}\right)\right)$$

Planarity is strictly preserved using 2D Axis-Aligned Bounding Box (AABB) fast rejection combined with the Jordan Curve orientation predicate, guaranteeing 0 unphysical edge crossings.

Step 3: Emergent Loop Engines

Mechanism A: Spatial Potential-Field Reconnection

Evaluates candidate pairs $(u, v)$ via KDTree nearest-neighbor queries: $$P_{\text{loop}}(u, v \mid R_{\text{rt}}) = \min(0.95, (P_0 + c_0 R_{\text{rt}}) \cdot e^{-d(u,v)/l_0} \cdot \max(0, \cos(\Delta \theta_{uv})))$$

Links are added under strict planarity and degree bounds ($k_{\text{in}} \le 2, k_{\text{out}} \le 3$). Maximum allowable loops scale with marine forcing: $$N_{\text{loops}}^{\max} = \lfloor N \cdot (\text{slope} \cdot R_{\text{rt}} + \text{intercept}) \rfloor$$

Mechanism B: Thermodynamic nER* Optimality

Constructs a candidate network and optimizes link configuration using Simulated Annealing to maximize Non-Local Shannon Entropy Rate ($nER^*$) under a total channel count budget.

Step 4: Pre-Hydraulic Network Pruning & Simplification

  • Inland Dead-End Pruning & Coastal Protection: Cascades through leaf nodes terminating inland without reaching the shoreline envelope: $$r < r_{\text{shore}} \cdot (1 - \epsilon_{\text{shore}})$$ where $\epsilon_{\text{shore}} = \text{outlet_tolerance}$ (default $0.05$). This accommodates coastal rugosity while guaranteeing 100% shoreline outlet delivery matching DIDNT observations.
  • Degree-2 Reach Simplification: Contracts non-branching pass-through chains ($d_{\text{in}}=1, d_{\text{out}}=1$) into direct links between morphological junctions. When recalculate_reach_sinuosity=True, a unified reach sinuosity is drawn from the lognormal process model rather than compounding discrete knees, matching RivGraph and DIDNT reach-scale standards. Continuous polyline coordinates are preserved across merged vertices.

Step 5: Emergent Hydraulics & Murray's Law Conservation

Channel widths are solved using an iterative topological relaxation solver conserving Murray's Law at all bifurcations: $$w_{\text{parent}}^p = w_{\text{child1}}^p + w_{\text{child2}}^p + \dots$$ where exponent $p \in [1.2, 2.2]$ (default $1.85$).

Estuarine Tidal Mouth Flaring

Tidal prism amplification widens distributary mouths seaward of an onset radius ($r_{\text{onset}} = \text{tidal_flare_onset}$, default $0.30$): $$w(r) = w_{\text{river}} \cdot \left((1 - R_{\text{rt}}) + R_{\text{rt}} \exp\left(k_{\text{flare}} \cdot \max\left(0, \frac{r}{r_{\text{shore}}} - r_{\text{onset}}\right)\right)\right)$$

Secondary Loop Resistance Penalty

To capture narrower cross-sections ($36\%-60\%$ of branch width) and higher floodplain bed elevations in anastomosing cross-ties (Gao et al. 2023), loop conductances are scaled by loop_flow_penalty (default $0.40$): $$C_{\text{loop}} = C_e \times \text{loop_flow_penalty}$$

Manning conductance scales as $C_e = w_e^\eta / L_e^\gamma$ (default $\eta = 1.65, \gamma = 0.50$).

Step 6: Curved Polyline Channel Geometry & Proximity Constraints

Meander amplitudes are constrained by lateral proximity to non-incident channels: $$A_{\max} = \max(0.0, d_{\text{clearance}} \cdot C_{\text{clear}})$$ $$S_{\max} = 1.0 + \left(\frac{\pi A_{\max}}{L}\right)^2$$

Centerline trajectories are generated via harmonic sine curves satisfying Dirichlet boundary conditions (zero displacement at nodes): $$y(s) = A \cdot \sin(2\pi \cdot \text{bends} \cdot s) \cdot \sin(\pi s)$$

When curved centerlines update reach length, hydraulic conductance $C_e = w^\eta / L^\gamma$ is automatically re-synchronized.

Step 7: Markov Flux Routing Solver & Metrics Battery

Steady-state water fluxes are solved via the transient submatrix formulation (Tejedor et al., 2017): $$(I - Q^T) \Phi_T = S_{0,T}$$ $$\Phi_A = S_{0,A} + W[T, A]^T \Phi_T$$

The 20-metric geomorphic battery computes: - Topological form: $\alpha$, $\beta_1/N$, $V_b$, $V_c$, $V_c/V_b$, $\Gamma$. - Entropy & Braiding: $nER^*$, $eBI$. - Energetic dissipation: Total dissipation $D\text{net}}$, variancdissipation. - Dynamic vulnerabily**: Global vulnerability $V_{\text{net}}$, max channel vulnerability. - Hydraulic geometry: Mean width, width CV, mean reach sinuosity, shoreline delivery ratio.

Multi-Stage Generative Pipeline Inspection

When dgm.generate(..., return_stages=True) is called, DGM captures and returns snapshots at key pipeline milestones: 1. 'tree': Polar tree backbone before loop closure. 2. 'looped': Network after spatial potential-field loop addition. 3. 'pruned': Simplified and pruned network before hydraulics. 4. 'final': Complete network with Murray widths, sinuous polylines, and Markov fluxes.

These snapshots can be directly rendered side-by-side using dgm.plot_stages or dgm.plot_tree_vs_network.
