DGM Model Parameters, Assumptions, & Literature Justifications

This document provides a comprehensive, field-by-field scientific reference for all physical constants, default parameters, underlying geomorphic assumptions, bounded sampling distributions, and algorithmic invariants supporting the DeltaGraphModel (DGM).

The document is organized into two primary parts: - Part I: Config-Tunable Parameters & Boundary Conditions (Sections 1–8), matching the exact structure and keys of config/default_config.yaml. Each parameter distinguishes natural empirical geomorphic foundations from calibrated modeling heuristics and algorithmic control flags. - Part II: Non-Tunable Geomorphic, Physical, & Algorithmic Invariants (Sections 9–12), documenting hardcoded physical scaling laws, numerical bounds, and topological constraints embedded in the model core.

Part I: Config-Tunable Parameters & Boundary Conditions

1. Global Model & Run Settings (model:)

Parameter (YAML Key) Type Default Value Valid Domain Geomorphic / Physical Assumption Scientific Status & Supporting Literature
N int 50 $[4, \inf]$ Discretization resolution and morphological junction/reach count of the delta network. Natural deltas span two orders of magnitude from micro-deltas ($N \approx 10^1$, e.g., Wairau, Po, Hay, Wax Lake) to mega-deltas ($N > 10^3$, e.g., Lena, Mackenzie, Mekong). Hariharan, Piliouras, Schwenk, & Passalacqua (2022), Width-Based Discharge Partitioning in Distributary Networks: How Right We Are, GRL, 49(14), e2022GL097897, doi:10.1029/2022GL097897; Schwenk & Hariharan (2021), RivGraph: Automatic extraction and analysis of river and delta channel network topology, JOSS, 6(59), 2952, doi:10.21105/joss.02952.
R_rt float 0.50 $[0.0, 1.0]$ Relative River-to-Tidal hydrodynamic forcing ratio. $R_{\text{rt}} = 0.0$ parameterizes river-dominated systems (elongated lobate fans, dendritic tree-like topology), while $R_{\text{rt}} = 1.0$ parameterizes tide-influenced deltas (wide flared mouths, high anastomosing cross-tie loops).

Modeling Note: In DGM, $R_{\text{rt}} \in [0, 1]$ specifically models the ascending limb of coastal delta loopiness up to intermediate tidal forcing ($T^* \sim 10^1 - 10^2$, Konkol et al. 2022). It intentionally simplifies the asymptotic collapse toward loopless dendritic trees observed in extreme macrotidal tidal marshes ($T^* > 10^3$) where river inputs are negligible. Furthermore, wave-driven shoreline smoothing (Broaddus et al. 2022) is distinct from tidal flaring and should not be conflated with $R_{\text{rt}}$.		 Conceptually Grounded in Galloway (1975) ternary classification; Broaddus, Vulis, Nienhuis, Tejedor, Brown, Foufoula-Georgiou, & Edmonds (2022), First-Order River Delta Morphology Is Explained by the Sediment Flux Balance From Rivers, Waves, and Tides, GRL, 49(23), e2022GL100355, doi:10.1029/2022GL100355; Nienhuis et al. (2020), Global-scale human impact on delta morphology has led to net land area gain, Nature, 577, 514–518, doi:10.1038/s41586-019-1905-9; Konkol, Schwenk, Katifori, & Shaw (2022), Interplay of River and Tidal Forcings Promotes Loops in Coastal Channel Networks, GRL, 49(10), e2022GL098284, doi:10.1029/2022GL098284.
loop_mechanism str "potential_field" ['potential_field', 'ner_optimality'] Reconnection engine selection. Mechanism A (potential_field) uses an engineered forward spatial potential field kernel ($P_{\text{loop}} \propto c_0 R_{\text{rt}} e^{-d/l_0} \cos\Delta\theta$) designed to replicate the tidal loop enhancement demonstrated by Konkol et al. (2022) without solving time-stepping vascular ODEs. Mechanism B (ner_optimality) prunes candidate loops via simulated annealing to maximize normalized flow entropy rate based on Tejedor et al. (2017). Mechanism A: Calibrated forward algorithmic proxy inspired by Konkol et al. (2022).
Mechanism B: Grounded in thermodynamic entropy optimality from Tejedor, Longjas, Edmonds, Zaliapin, Georgiou, Rinaldo, & Foufoula-Georgiou (2017), Entropy and optimality in river deltas, PNAS, 114(44), 11651–11656, doi:10.1073/pnas.1708404114.
sample_parameters bool false [true, false] Governs whether active parameters are fixed to default values (deterministic realization) or drawn from empirical distributions in distributions: (stochastic cloud ensemble representing natural geomorphic variability). -
seed int 42 Any Integer / null Ensures exact algorithmic reproducibility of stochastic graph realisations, bifurcation angles, and spatial branching geometry. -

2. Polar Domain Geometry (domain:)

Parameter (YAML Key) Type Default Value Valid Domain Geomorphic / Physical Assumption Scientific Status & Supporting Literature
r_shore float 10000.0 m $> 0$ m Radial distance from the delta apex ($r=0$) to the receiving marine shoreline. Establishes the characteristic spatial scale ($L_{\text{delta}}$) of the delta plain. Natural apex-to-shoreline radii span $\sim 2\text{ km}$ (Mossy Delta), $\sim 10\text{ km}$ (Wax Lake Delta), up to $40\text{--}60\text{ km}$ (Colville, Danube, Lena). Default $10\text{ km}$ represents intermediate mesotidal/microtidal bayhead deltas. Hariharan et al. (2022) (DIDNT global dataset), GRL, doi:10.1029/2022GL097897; Syvitski et al. (2009), Sinking deltas due to human activities, Nature Geosci, 2, 681–686, doi:10.1038/ngeo629.
theta_base float 65.0° $> 0$° Baseline opening angle of unconfined fluvial-dominated deltas. In river-dominated unconfined fans, forward progradation and avulsion switching produce fan opening angles of $60^\circ - 80^\circ$. Coffey & Shaw (2017), GRL, doi:10.1002/2017GL074873 ($\mu = 70.4^\circ$ at apex); Cazanacli (2021), PhD Thesis, pp. 18, 60 ($48^\circ - 55^\circ$ reach trajectories); Galloway (1975); Syvitski & Saito (2007).
theta_delta float 60.0° $\ge 0$° Marine/tidal lateral spreading capacity. Tidal currents and waves redistribute sediment alongshore and across estuarine funnels, expanding the planform fan opening angle from $45^\circ$ up to $105^\circ - 135^\circ$ ($\theta_{\text{open}} = \theta_{\text{base}} + R_{\text{rt}} \cdot \theta_{\text{delta}}$). Galloway (1975); Orton & Reading (1993), Variability of deltaic processes in terms of sediment supply, with particular emphasis on grain size, Sedimentology, 40(3), 475–512; Sassi et al. (2012), JGR Earth Surface, doi:10.1029/2012JF002448.
confinement ($C_{\text{conf}}$) float 0.0 $[0.0, 0.95]$ Degree of lateral valley/topographic confinement ($0.0 = \text{open coastal plain}$; $>0.8 = \text{incised bedrock graben or levee valley}$). Compresses opening angle: $\theta = \theta_0 (1 - C_{\text{conf}})$. Dong, Vulis, Ma, Tejedor, Goudge, & Passalacqua (2022), Apparent Hack's Law in River Deltas, pp. 4–5 (57% of uniform deltas are valley-confined, vs 80% of composite deltas unconfined); Marra et al. (2014), Alluvial fan confinement, GSA Bull.
lateral_valley_width ($W_{\text{valley}}$) float null $> 0$ m Physical valley corridor width cutoff (meters), clamping lateral channel spreading: $\|y(r)\| \le W_{\text{valley}} / 2$. Represents lateral valley walls, fault scarps, or bedrock lineaments. Gibling & Rust (1993), Alluvial architecture in confined basins, Basin Research, 5(3), 133–149; Dong et al. (2022).

3. Polar Tree Backbone Growth (backbone:)

Parameter (YAML Key) Type Default Value Valid Domain Geomorphic / Physical Assumption Scientific Status & Supporting Literature
branching_weights tuple [0.50, 0.45, 0.05] Normalized simplex Probabilities of generating $\{1, 2, 3\}$ children at each tree extension step. Captures the empirical frequency of single continuation reaches ($\sim 50\%$) vs binary bifurcations ($\sim 45\%$) vs rare trifurcations ($\sim 5\%$). Calibrated Algorithmic Queue Distribution. Calibrated to produce dominant binary branching ($k=2$) and rare mouth-bar trifurcations ($k=3$, $\sim 5\%$), maintaining single-continuation reaches ($\sim 50\%$) for node density pacing. Grounded in topological binary branching dominance from Smart (1971), Quantitative Properties of Delta Channel Networks, IBM Tech Report #3, AD0719918; Cazanacli (2021), PhD Thesis; and Edmonds & Slingerland (2007), JGR Earth Surface, doi:10.1029/2006JF000574.
radial_step_scale_min float 0.65 $> 0$ Minimum radial step multiplier between successive bifurcation points, preventing unphysical crystallographic grid-like lattice spacing. Heuristic Jitter Bounds. Numerical scaling jitter ($\pm 35\%$ around r_step_base) to avoid concentric rings. Conceptually aligned with continuous random walk stepping in Cazanacli (2021) and reach length distributions in Passalacqua et al. (2013).
radial_step_scale_max float 1.35 $\ge \text{step\_min}$ Maximum radial step multiplier between successive bifurcation points. Heuristic Jitter Bounds.
coastal_node_decay ($\lambda_{\text{node}}$) float 0.40 $[0.0, 0.80]$ Shoreward spatial contraction factor for node spacing: $\Delta r(r) = \Delta r_0 (1 - \lambda_{\text{node}} r / r_{\text{shore}})$. With successive splits, per-channel discharge drops, reducing jet momentum and shortening the distance to mouth-bar formation downstream.

Model Caveat: While mouth-bar spacing decreases downstream under jet theory (Edmonds & Slingerland 2007), Cazanacli (2021, pp. 25–26) and Smart (1971, p. 13) found that reach lengths follow an exponential distribution across whole deltas without universal downstream contraction. This parameter is an active morphodynamic modeling choice.		 Theory-Motivated Modeling Choice. Edmonds & Slingerland (2007), JGR Earth Surface, doi:10.1029/2006JF000574; Dong et al. (2022); Vulis et al. (2020), GRL, doi:10.1029/2019GL086710.
angular_spread_decay float 0.70 $> 0$ Attenuation of lateral branching deflection at deeper bifurcations. Prevents channels from diverging unnaturally out of the delta fan corridor: $\text{spread\_base} = (\theta_{\text{open}} / 2.5) / \max(1.0, 0.70(\text{level}+1))$.

Model Caveat: Coffey & Shaw (2017, Fig. 3b) observed narrowing along an individual island ($L/W_0 \le 2$), not across successive tree generations. Decaying angles across hierarchical tree levels is an engineered geometric constraint to enforce fan containment.		 Engineered Geometric Constraint. Conceptually motivated by downstream channel alignment (Coffey & Shaw 2017, doi:10.1002/2017GL074873; Cazanacli 2021, pp. 18, 60), but implemented across tree depth to guarantee fan containment.
single_branch_spread_factor float 0.35 $(0.0, 1.0]$ Angular deflection scaling applied to single-continuation (degree-1 extension) reaches relative to full bifurcation spread ($\sigma_\theta \approx 2^\circ - 4^\circ$). Heuristic Deflection Scaler. Calibrated to match small reach directional deflections ($\sigma_\theta \approx 2^\circ - 4^\circ$, $C_d \approx 0.8$) documented in Cazanacli (2021), PhD Thesis, pp. 17–19.
bifurcation_spread_min float 0.60 $> 0$ Minimum bifurcation angle scaling factor ($\theta_{\text{bif}} \approx 40^\circ - 75^\circ$). Natural deltas exhibit an incipient apex bifurcation angle of $\mu = 70.4^\circ \pm 2.6^\circ$ ($\sigma = 18.6^\circ$), narrowing downstream to an effective reach-scale trajectory of $48^\circ \pm 2.5^\circ$. Empirically Grounded Scaling Envelope. Coffey & Shaw (2017), GRL, doi:10.1002/2017GL074873 ($n=197$, $\mu = 70.4^\circ$, $\sigma = 18.6^\circ$); Cazanacli (2021), PhD Thesis, pp. 18, 60 ($48^\circ \pm 2.5^\circ$ reach trajectory); Edmonds & Slingerland (2007, 2010), Nature Geosci, doi:10.1038/ngeo958.
bifurcation_spread_max float 1.40 $\ge \text{spread\_min}$ Maximum bifurcation angle scaling factor, capturing the upper empirical envelope of natural delta bifurcations ($\sim 75^\circ - 85^\circ$). Empirically Grounded Scaling Envelope. Coffey & Shaw (2017); Cazanacli (2021).
base_sinuosity_mean ($\mu_{\text{sin}}$) float 0.25 $\ge 0.0$ Log-normal location parameter $\mu$ for reach sinuosity ($S = L / D \ge 1.0$), with a median of $\sim 1.28$.

Model Caveat: Sampling unshifted $\text{Lognormal}(0.25, 0.15)$ yields values $< 1.0$ approximately $4.8\%$ of the time, requiring an ad-hoc max(1.0, ...) clamp. Furthermore, while $1.28$ is typical for inland meandering rivers, distributary reaches in delta backwater zones are typically straighter ($S \approx 1.05 - 1.20$, Cazanacli 2021, p. 22).		 Empirically Motivated Baseline. Leopold & Wolman (1957), USGS Prof. Paper 282-B; Langbein & Leopold (1966), USGS Prof. Paper 422-H; Finotello et al. (2024), Geological Society Special Publications, doi:10.1144/SP540-2023-53; Cazanacli (2021), p. 22.
base_sinuosity_std ($\sigma_{\text{sin}}$) float 0.15 $> 0.0$ Log-normal scale parameter $\sigma$ for reach sinuosity distribution. Leopold & Wolman (1957); Finotello et al. (2024).
coastal_sinuosity_decay ($\lambda_{\text{sin}}$) float 0.30 $[0.0, 0.90]$ Shoreward channel straightening: distributaries straighten as they approach the coast ($S \to 1.0$) due to bedload sedimentation and backwater hydrodynamics. Empirically Supported Phenomenon, Heuristic Equation. Straightening documented by Cazanacli (2021, p. 22) citing Nittrouer et al. (2012) and Fernandes et al. (2016); the linear formula 1.0 - lambda * r_frac is an invented modeling heuristic. (Note: Sassi et al. 2012 studied width flaring, not sinuosity).

4. Emergent Loop Engine (loops:)

Parameter (YAML Key) Type Default Value Valid Domain Geomorphic / Physical Assumption Scientific Status & Supporting Literature
candidate_dist_scale ($l_0 / r_{\text{shore}}$) float 0.25 $> 0$ Characteristic spatial correlation length for loop formation. Reconnection probability decays exponentially with inter-channel distance: $\propto \exp(-d / l_0)$. Interdistributary connecting channels typically span $20\% - 30\%$ of branch lengths. Calibrated Algorithmic Spatial Scaler. Calibrated heuristic ensuring candidate pairs have viable reconnection probabilities across typical inter-channel distances. Conceptually motivated by tidal loop zones in Konkol et al. (2022) and tie-channel dimensions in Gao et al. (2023), doi:10.1029/2022WR033714.
reconnection_prob_base ($c_0$) float 0.55 $\ge 0$ Base cross-tie reconnection scaling factor tied directly to marine tidal forcing: $P_{\text{loop}} \propto c_0 \cdot R_{\text{rt}}$. High tidal energy stabilizes loops against runaway single-branch dominance. Calibrated Algorithmic Multiplier. Calibrated probability multiplier designed to produce tree-reduction fractions ($\Omega \approx 0.20 - 0.40$) matching field deltas at intermediate tidal forcing ($T^* \sim 10^1$, Konkol et al. 2022, GRL, doi:10.1029/2022GL098284).
anastomosis_prob_base ($P_0$) float 0.0 $\ge 0$ Baseline inland avulsion/floodplain reconnection rate present even in purely fluvial regimes without tides.

Model Note: Setting default $P_0 = 0.0$ isolates tidal looping by forcing $P_{\text{loop}} = 0$ when $R_{\text{rt}} = 0$. For deltas with significant fluvial tie-channels (e.g. Dongjiang, upper Paraná, Columbia), setting $P_0 \in [0.05, 0.15]$ is recommended.		 Configurable Physical Baseline. Supported by fluvial tie-channel studies: Gao et al. (2023), WRR, doi:10.1029/2022WR033714; Makaske (2001), Earth-Sci Rev, doi:10.1016/S0012-8252(00)00038-6.
max_in_degree ($k_{\text{in}}^{\max}$) int 2 $\ge 1$ Maximum channel confluence order: rivers rarely coalesce in order $>2$ without forming an intermediate lake or wide bay node. Strongly Backed by Physical Geomorphology. Smart (1971), Quantitative Properties of Delta Channel Networks, IBM Tech Report #3, AD0719918; Schumm (1972), The Fluvial System.
max_out_degree ($k_{\text{out}}^{\max}$) int 3 $\ge 1$ Maximum stable bifurcation order at a single junction (predominantly binary bifurcations, rarely ternary splits $\sim 5\%$). Strongly Backed by Physical Geomorphology. Smart (1971); Cazanacli (2021); Edmonds & Slingerland (2007).
target_max_loops_slope float 0.22 - Slope for maximum loop count ceiling during simulated annealing in Mechanism B ($nER^*$ optimization): $\text{max\_loops} = \text{round}((0.05 + 0.22 \cdot R_{\text{rt}}) \cdot N)$. Synthetic Optimization Ceiling. Formulated for DGM Mechanism B to prevent simulated annealing from over-connecting the planar mesh.
target_max_loops_intercept float 0.05 - Intercept for maximum loop count ceiling in Mechanism B. Synthetic Optimization Ceiling.
sa_initial_temp float 1.0 $> 0$ Initial thermodynamic annealing temperature for simulated annealing loop pruning in Mechanism B. Algorithmic Optimization Hyperparameter. Standard simulated annealing schedule from Kirkpatrick, Gelatt, & Vecchi (1983), Optimization by simulated annealing, Science, 220(4598), 671–680. (Note: Tejedor et al. 2017 provided the target entropy metric, not simulated annealing).
sa_cooling_rate ($\alpha$) float 0.975 $(0, 1)$ Geometric cooling schedule multiplier: $T_{k+1} = \alpha T_k$. Algorithmic Optimization Hyperparameter. Kirkpatrick et al. (1983).
sa_max_steps int 250 $\ge 1$ Maximum iteration budget for simulated annealing thermodynamic loop pruning. Algorithmic Optimization Hyperparameter.

5. Pre-Hydraulic Network Pruning & Simplification (pruning:)

Parameter (YAML Key) Type Default Value Valid Domain Geomorphic / Physical Assumption Scientific Status & Supporting Literature
enable_pruning bool false [true, false] Orchestrates pre-hydraulic dead-end removal and topological chain contraction before width/flux calculations. Algorithmic Control Flag. Feature flag for network simplification matching the RivGraph pipeline standard (Schwenk & Hariharan 2021, JOSS, doi:10.21105/joss.02952).
prune_inland_dead_ends bool true [true, false] Eliminates blind inland channel fingers that fail to reach the coast or reconnect, ensuring 100% shoreline outlet delivery matching physical deltas. Hariharan, Piliouras, Schwenk, & Passalacqua (2022), GRL, doi:10.1029/2022GL097897; Schwenk & Hariharan (2021), JOSS.
outlet_tolerance ($\epsilon_{\text{shore}}$) float 0.05 $[0.001, 0.50]$ Proximity tolerance fraction defining legitimate shoreline outlets: $r \ge r_{\text{shore}} (1 - \epsilon_{\text{shore}})$. Accounts for shoreline rugosity, progradation irregularity, and coastal island crenulations. Discretization Tolerance Buffer. Accounts for coastal rugosity and prevents near-shore distributaries from being misclassified as inland dead-ends (Schwenk & Hariharan 2021; Syvitski et al. 2009).
min_spur_length float 0.0 m $\ge 0.0$ m Length threshold below which dead-end channel fingers are considered ephemeral depositional scars or image noise and pruned. ($0.0 = \text{all inland dead-ends}$). Empirically Supported Filtering Threshold. Default $0.0$ removes all blind spurs. In ensemble distributions ($[0, 500\text{ m}]$), it filters unresolved sub-scale channels matching Wortmann et al. (2025), Global River Topology (GRIT), WRR, 61, e2024WR038308, Section 2.6 (short dangles $<500\text{ m}$ removed).
simplify_pass_through_nodes bool false [true, false] Contraction of degree-2 pass-through nodes ($d_{\text{in}}=1, d_{\text{out}}=1$) into direct links between morphological junctions, accumulating path length for accurate reach sinuosity ($S = \sum L / D \ge 1.0$). Matches RivGraph delineation standard. Fully Grounded in Network Delineation Standards. Schwenk & Hariharan (2021), JOSS, doi:10.21105/joss.02952; Smart (1971), IBM Tech Report #3.
recalculate_reach_sinuosity bool true [true, false] When contracting degree-2 chains, recalculates a single unified reach-scale sinuosity directly from the process model lognormal distribution rather than compounding discrete intermediate knees, preventing reach sinuosity inflation and matching RivGraph / DIDNT conventions. Methodological Alignment with Field Reach Standards. Prevents compounding discretization artifacts during topological contraction (Schwenk & Hariharan 2021; Hariharan et al., 2022, GRL).
max_prune_iterations int 5 $\ge 1$ Safe upper bound on recursive cascading dead-end spur removals. -

6. Emergent Hydraulics & Markov Flux Routing (hydraulics:)

Parameter (YAML Key) Type Default Value Valid Domain Geomorphic / Physical Assumption Scientific Status & Supporting Literature
routing_mode str "width" ['width', 'conductance'] Flow partition weighting at bifurcations: proportional to channel width power ($w^\eta$) or hydrodynamic conductance ($w^\eta / L^\gamma$). Partitioning discharge by linear average channel width ($\overline{W}^1$) is unbiased and robust across 162 field channels and 46 bifurcations, performing on par with or better than complex non-linear hydraulic exponents in uncalibrated settings. Strongly Backed by Empirical Field Testing. Hariharan, Piliouras, Schwenk, & Passalacqua (2022), Width-Based Discharge Partitioning in Distributary Networks: How Right We Are, GRL, 49(14), e2022GL097897, doi:10.1029/2022GL097897; Tejedor et al. (2015a), WRR, doi:10.1002/2014WR016577.
murray_p ($p$) float 1.85 $> 0$ Murray's Law width conservation exponent: $w_{\text{parent}}^p = w_{\text{child1}}^p + w_{\text{child2}}^p$. $p \approx 1.5 - 2.0$ (default $1.85$) balances frictional dissipation and sediment transport in alluvial open channels, corresponding to downstream hydraulic geometry width exponent $b \approx 0.54$ ($p = 1/b \approx 1.85$), yielding $+37.5\%$ cumulative width expansion and $+7.8\%$ hydraulic conveyance gain under Manning flow ($\eta=1.65$). Theoretically Grounded in Hydraulic Geometry. Murray (1926), The physiological principle of minimum work, PNAS, 12(3), 207–214; Leopold & Langbein (1962), USGS Prof. Paper 500-A; Gleason (2015); Rodriguez-Iturbe & Rinaldo (1997); Sood et al. (2020). (Note: Hariharan et al. 2022 tested bifurcation flow partitioning, not Murray width conservation).
width_beta ($\beta$) float 0.745 $(0.0, 1.0)$ Bifurcation asymmetry factor governing primary vs secondary child channel width partition: $w_1 = \beta w_{\text{sym}}$, $w_2 = (1 - \beta^p)^{1/p} w_{\text{parent}}$. Field bifurcation measurements across 46 bifurcations confirm mean flow asymmetry $\Psi = |Q_a - Q_b| / (Q_a + Q_b)$ is centered near $0.3 - 0.5$, supporting primary branch width shares of $\sim 70\% - 80\%$. Calibrated Empirical Target. Calibrated to match the median field flow asymmetry ($\Psi = |2\beta - 1| \approx 0.49$) documented in Hariharan et al. (2022), GRL, doi:10.1029/2022GL097897 and Buschman et al. (2010); physical splitting physics from Edmonds & Slingerland (2007) and Kleinhans et al. (2008).
manning_eta ($\eta$) float 1.65 $> 0$ Manning hydraulic conductance width exponent ($Q \propto w \cdot h \cdot v \propto w \cdot h^{5/3} \propto w^{1.65}$ with bankfull hydraulic geometry $h \propto w^{0.4}$). Analytically Derived from Open-Channel Hydraulics. Derived from Manning (1891) and hydraulic geometry depth scaling ($h \propto w^{0.4}$, Dingman 2009, Physical Hydrology). (Note: Tejedor et al. 2015a used pure width $w^1$, acknowledging that exponents like $\eta$ can be incorporated for detailed modeling).
length_gamma ($\gamma$) float 0.50 $> 0$ Channel length friction damping exponent in hydraulic conductance ($C_e = w^\eta / L^\gamma$). Analytically Derived from Slope Scaling. Derived from slope head-loss scaling $S = \Delta z / L \implies v \propto S^{1/2} \propto L^{-0.5}$ (Paola et al. 1992).
apex_width ($w_{\text{apex}}$) float 250.0 m $> 0$ m Bankfull channel width of the trunk river entering the delta apex. Across the 28 deltas in DIDNT, apex feeder widths range from $50\text{ m}$ to $>500\text{ m}$ (Mississippi $\sim 500\text{ m}$, Wax Lake $\sim 300\text{ m}$, Colville $\sim 150\text{ m}$). Default $250\text{ m}$ represents intermediate systems. Empirically Grounded. Hariharan et al. (2022) (DIDNT dataset), GRL, doi:10.1029/2022GL097897; Syvitski & Saito (2007).
width_noise ($\sigma_w$) float 0.30 $\ge 0$ Log-normal noise parameter modeling bank cohesiveness, vegetation anchoring, and local gravel/mud erodibility heterogeneity. Heuristic Stochastic Dispersion. Represents natural width heterogeneity driven by riparian cohesion (Edmonds & Slingerland 2010, Nature Geosci; Tal & Paola 2007, Science). $\sigma_w = 0.30$ is an uncalibrated synthetic noise parameter.
tidal_flare_k ($k_{\text{flare}}$) float 1.40 $\ge 0$ Exponential seaward flaring rate of distributary mouths under tidal prism amplification: $w(r) = w_0 \exp(k_{\text{flare}} R_{\text{rt}} \cdot \max(0, r/r_{\text{shore}} - r_{\text{onset}}))$. Back-calculated from the $\sim 4\times$ width flaring observed in the Mahakam delta ($k_{\text{flare}} \approx \ln(4.0) / 0.70 \approx 1.40$) within an exponential estuarine framework. Calibrated Empirical Fit. Calibrated to match the $\sim 4\times$ estuarine mouth flaring documented in Sassi, Hoitink, de Brye, & Deleersnijder (2012), Downstream hydraulic geometry of a tidally influenced river delta, JGR Earth Surface, 117, F04022, doi:10.1029/2012JF002448; exponential estuarine geometry from Wright & Coleman (1973) and Savenije (2005).
tidal_flare_onset ($r_{\text{onset}}$) float 0.30 $[0.0, 1.0)$ Seaward normalized radial distance ($r / r_{\text{shore}}$) where tidal backwater flaring initiates.

Documented Discrepancy: Sassi et al. (2012) identified the empirical along-channel morphological breakpoint at $s/L = 0.55$ and hydrodynamic breakpoint at $s/L = 0.58 - 0.65$. The DGM default $r_{\text{onset}} = 0.30$ on radial distance initiates flaring significantly earlier in the delta plain to enable smooth, gradual seaward backwater widening across the distal 70% of the plain.		 Intentional Synthetic Tuning. Calibrated modeling choice enabling gradual distal widening across the lower plain; differs from the sharper empirical breakpoint ($s/L = 0.55$) in Sassi et al. (2012).
loop_flow_penalty float 0.40 $(0.0, 1.0]$ Relative hydraulic conveyance factor applied to secondary cross-tie loops ($w \propto \text{penalty}^{1/p}$). Reflects higher bed elevation, narrower cross-sections ($36\% - 60\%$ of branch width), and increased vegetation resistance of floodplain tie-channels. Empirically Grounded. Gao, Wang, Kleinhans, Miao, Cui, & Shao (2023), Floodplain Connecting Channels as Critical Paths for Hydrological Connectivity of Deltaic River Networks, WRR, 59(4), e2022WR033714, doi:10.1029/2022WR033714 (Table 1 width ratios $36\% - 60\%$); Makaske (2001).
min_channel_width float 0.1 m $> 0$ m Physical numerical cutoff preventing singular zero-width channels. -
relaxation_max_iter int 15 $\ge 1$ Max iterations for iterative Murray's Law relaxation solver across cyclic loops and confluences. -
relaxation_tol float 1e-4 $> 0$ Relative tolerance for Murray's Law width conservation convergence. -
